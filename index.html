Affirmative Case Construction

Master the structure, terminology, and flowing techniques required to build an impenetrable affirmative case in Lincoln Douglas debate.

Core Definitions

Hover over or tap the cards to reveal the definitions.

Criterion

A measuring stick for the value; a uniform standard to see how much of the value we are achieving.

Contention

A short, firm statement that directly relates and supports your position, containing a tag line and content.

Introduction

The beginning of the case structure that hooks the judge and smoothly states the resolution verbatim.

Value
Justification

The reasoning behind the choice of value; expands on the definition and links it to the resolution.

Criterion
Justification

A section explaining why the criterion is an appropriate measuring stick for the value.

Case Structure

A predetermined configuration/outline that helps a debater define and defend their position.

Alternate
Case Structure

Varying ways to present a case structure reserved for advanced debaters, aiming to accomplish the same objectives.

Flowing

A note-taking system debaters use to follow argumentation horizontally throughout the round.

NC

Negative Constructive (the column where negative responds to affirmative and builds their case).

2AR

Second Affirmative Rebuttal (the final speech where the affirmative reviews arguments and offers final refutation).
